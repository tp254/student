Strategy notes

Bio gets top priority
Bio is May 4 — your first exam and one of your two weakest subjects. Physics C doesn't test until May 13, so you get a dedicated 9-day sprint after Bio finishes. Sequence matters here.
The rotation problem
Rotational dynamics (torque, moment of inertia, angular momentum, rolling without slipping) shows up in nearly every Physics C FRQ. This plan revisits it every week from Week 3 onward — don't skip those sessions even on robotics days.
CSP Create task
Due April 30 — before exams open. If you haven't started, this week: outline the program and write a first draft of all three written response prompts. Small progress every Wed/Sat beats a weekend panic.
Robotics days (R)
On R days, budget ~1.5 hrs total. Pick the single highest-priority task from one subject and go deep. Do not try to cover two subjects. Quality beats quantity when you're tired from build season.
Book strategy
Barron's for content review — thorough chapter structure, clear explanations. Princeton Review for realistic practice tests. AP Classroom (collegeboard.org, free) for authentic past FRQs — these are the gold standard for both Bio and Physics C.
May 13 is the hardest day
Eng Lang morning, Physics C afternoon — two exams one day. The upside: Eng Lang needs minimal prep. The week before May 13 is essentially a Physics C sprint with one light 45-min Eng Lang session mixed in.
